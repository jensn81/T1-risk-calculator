About this calculator

This calculator compares two management strategies for T1 rectal cancer following local excision (EID/ESD/TEM/TAMIS): completion total mesorectal excision (cTME/APR) versus structured surveillance with a low threshold for salvage treatment.

All default values are derived from published studies and meta-analyses. The default profile is DSI alone – the patient group in which the indication for cTME is most actively debated.

Risk presets

  • DSI alone: LNM 2.6%, LR-surveillance 8%, DM 2% — Zwager 2022; van Oostendorp 2020
  • Low risk: LNM 1%, LR-surveillance 6.7%, DM 1.5% — van Oostendorp 2020 low-risk pT1
  • Intermediate (1 factor): LNM 8%, LR-surveillance 10%, DM 2.5%
  • High risk (≥2 factors): LNM 20%, LR-surveillance 20%, DM 5% — van Oostendorp 2020 high-risk pT1

Key assumptions

  • Distant metastases are assumed equal in both groups — supported by Ouchi 2024, Gijsbers 2024, van Oostendorp 2020
  • The age gradient for surgical mortality follows Waddison et al. 2011 (colorectal surgery, elective)
  • Salvage after cTME is not separately modelled (small group, lower probability of curative salvage)
  • Cancer-specific survival benefit of cTME is ~1.8 percentage points (Ouchi 2024) — not explicitly modelled but reflected in the LR/DM estimates

Limitations

  • Simplified model — for illustration, not for individual clinical decisions
  • Does not incorporate rectal location as an independent risk factor for recurrence
  • Functional outcomes (LARS, stoma) are binary estimates — actual patient experience varies widely
  • All estimates carry confidence intervals — the calculator uses point estimates

Context

The calculator was developed as part of a review of the evidence base for the Norwegian national guidelines on T1 colorectal cancer management (2025–2026). It reflects a systematic assessment of the evidence supporting organ-preserving strategies in patients with deep submucosal invasion (DSI) as the sole histopathological risk factor.

References

  1. Zwager LW et al. Gastroenterology 2022. Deep submucosal invasion is not an independent risk factor for lymph node metastasis in T1 colorectal cancer: a meta-analysis. DOI: 10.1053/j.gastro.2022.04.010
    DSI alone: LNM 2.6% (n=1,146, 8 studies). DSI multivariate OR 1.73 (95%CI 0.96–3.12, p=NS). LVI OR 3.16, tumour budding OR 2.83, poor differentiation OR 2.14.
  2. van Oostendorp SE et al. BJS 2020. Local recurrence after local excision of early rectal cancer: a meta-analysis of completion TME, adjuvant (chemo)radiation, or no additional treatment. DOI: 10.1002/bjs.12040
    Low-risk pT1 NAT: LR 6.7%, DM 3.2%. High-risk pT1 NAT: LR 13.6%, DM 7.2%. cTME: LR 4.1%, DM 5.6%. Salvage eligible 73–93%.
  3. Ouchi A et al. Ann Surg 2024. How does omitting additional surgery after local excision affect the prognostic outcome of patients with high-risk T1 colorectal cancer? DOI: 10.1097/SLA.0000000000006092
    PS-matched n=401 pairs. 5-year LR: 1.1% vs 4.1%. DM: 1.3% vs 1.8% (p=0.56). 5-year CSS: 99.7% vs 97.9% (p=0.048). OS difference confounded by comorbidity.
  4. Corre F et al. GIE 2023. Impact of surgery after endoscopically resected high-risk T1 CRC: results of an emulated target trial. DOI: 10.1016/j.gie.2023.09.027
    n=197, 14 French centres, IPTW. Recurrence: 3.7% surgery vs 4.4% surveillance. wHR 0.95 (95%CI 0.52–1.75), NS.
  5. Gijsbers K et al. ESGE Days 2024 (conference abstract, under peer review). n=3,812, 21 Dutch hospitals.
    69.7% of distant metastases after surgery in pN0 patients. 3-year AFS high-risk: 96.0% vs 95.6%, HR 0.93 (NS). DSI not included as independent predictor of DM.
  6. Rönnow CF et al. Ann Surg 2022. Lymphovascular infiltration, not depth of invasion, is the critical risk factor of metastases in early colorectal cancer. DOI: 10.1097/SLA.0000000000003854
    n=1,439, exclusively non-pedunculated lesions, Danish validation cohort, multiple imputation. DSI p=0.075 (NS). LVI OR 7.3 (p<0.001).
  7. Nilsson J et al. BJS Open 2024. Swedish national registry, T1 colon cancer, n=1,805. DOI: 10.1093/bjsopen/zrae053
    Recurrence: Sm1 3.7% / Sm2 3.8% / Sm3 3.2% (NS). LVI HR 3.73. 5-year DFI: 95.6% vs 96.2% (NS).
  8. Kajiwara Y et al. GIE 2023. Nomogram as a novel predictive tool for LNM in T1 CRC. DOI: 10.1016/j.gie.2023.01.022
    n=4,673, 27 Japanese centres. DSI ≥2000 µm OR ~4.3. Limitation: ~23.5% missing key variables, complete-case analysis only, no multiple imputation.
  9. Vermeer NCA et al. BJS Open 2019. National cohort study on postoperative risks after surgery for submucosal invasive colorectal cancer (DCRA, n=5,170). DOI: 10.1002/bjs5.50125
    30-day surgical mortality 1.7%. Severe morbidity 8.3% – comparable to pT2–3 disease.
  10. Chaouch MA et al. Meta-analysis 2021. Surgical mortality cTME: 1.2% (95%CI 0.6–2.4%). Morbidity 36% (32–40%).
  11. Nikberg M et al. (HAPIrect RCT). BJS Open. Hartmann's procedure vs intersphincteric APE, n=163 randomised. NCT01995396.
    Overall complication rate 45–49%. No significant difference between techniques.
  12. Croese AD et al. Int J Surg 2018. Meta-analysis of the prevalence of Low Anterior Resection Syndrome. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijsu.2018.06.031
    Major LARS: 41% (95%CI 34–48%). Low anastomotic height and radiotherapy are the strongest risk factors.
  13. Waddison B et al. Ann R Coll Surg Engl 2011. The impact of age on outcome after surgery for colorectal adenocarcinoma (n=459). DOI: 10.1308/003588411x587154
    30-day mortality: 1% (<59 years), +3 percentage points per decade. Cancer-specific survival independent of age.
  14. Norwegian NPR data 2016–2025 (under publication). n=299 major rectal resections for benign rectal lesions.
    90-day complications 39.1%, reoperation 11.7%, 90-day mortality 2.0%, stoma 26.7% (62.5% reversed within 6 months).
  15. van der Schee L et al. GUT 2025. Prospective cohort, n=188 EID (2019–2023).
    All recurrences in low/intermediate risk group were salvageable. 0% cancer-related mortality at 3 years.
  16. Dekkers N et al. Surg Endosc 2022 (PMID 35773606). Meta-analysis, pT1 rectal cancer, n=2,585.
    High-risk pT1 recurrence: flexible endoscopy 12.5% vs TEM/TAMIS 29.7%. Low-risk: 3.1% vs 5.9%.
